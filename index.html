        Picking rules

        Anyone may pick wild mushrooms in Swiss woods in usual local amounts (Art. 699 ZGB), but cantons and communes set the rules. Many limit how much each person may pick per day, some have closed days, and picking is often banned in nature reserves. Gardens, fenced and private land are off limits.

        Check the rules of the canton and commune before you go. VAPKO overview of cantonal rules ↗ (2012, so confirm with the canton).

        Cut or twist carefully, leave small and old specimens, and don't trample the area.

        How this works

        Finds come from GBIF, which collects verified iNaturalist photos and Swiss survey records. Only the edible species in the guide are fetched. Records reach GBIF about two to three weeks after they are posted.

        Fruiting score (0 to 100) uses MET Norway forecasts for nine regions: Zürich, Basel, Bern, Luzern, St. Gallen, Lausanne, Sion, Lugano and Chur. Each canton uses the region nearest its capital, so valleys and high ground can differ a lot. Up to 60 points come from rain over the previous 10 days (30 mm or more scores full marks). Up to 40 points come from the day's mean temperature, best between 8 and 16 °C. Frost in the last three days takes off 25 to 40 points. Most flushes appear 5 to 10 days after a good soaking.

        Range: MET Norway forecasts about 9 to 10 days ahead. Scores past day 5 are much less reliable.

        Photos are example images from Wikimedia Commons, credited on each one. They are not an identification guide.

        Updates run four times a day. Rain from past days is kept from earlier forecasts. Regions other than Zürich started their record on 20 Oct 2026, so their first scores run low.
